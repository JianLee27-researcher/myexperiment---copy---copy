Content fixes: HR due-diligence framing, 7-point scale unification, neutral round-repeat rationale

HUMAN RIGHTS DUE DILIGENCE LABELING
- Relabeled "Human Rights" -> "Human Rights Due Diligence" / "HR Due
  Diligence" across Briefing.html, RoleAssignment.html, SADecision.html.
  Paper 1's methodology explicitly defines this criterion as the supplier's
  level of above-compliance due-diligence assurance (ILO alignment, audit
  coverage, grievance mechanisms) — NOT presence/absence of violations. The
  old plain "Human Rights" label risked participants misreading a low score
  as "this supplier violates human rights" rather than the intended
  above-compliance construct.
- Added an explanatory note (Briefing.html + both SADecision.html score
  tables): all candidate suppliers already meet baseline legal human rights
  requirements; the score reflects additional assurance practices only.
- PADecision.html untouched — PA never sees CSR criteria, so no mention of
  this label existed there.

TRUST SURVEY SCALE UNIFICATION
- mc_transparency_1 / mc_transparency_2 changed from a 5-point scale
  ([0..5]) to a 7-point scale ([0..7]), matching every other trust item in
  the survey (team, interpersonal, McKnight reliability/functionality/
  helpfulness). No established/validated instrument was being preserved by
  the 5-point range — it was an unintentional inconsistency.
- Removed now-unused scale_5 / scale_5_na template vars from TrustSurvey's
  vars_for_template.
- TrustSurvey.html Section 6 label updated: "5 = Strongly Agree" ->
  "7 = Strongly Agree".

ROUND-REPEAT RATIONALE — BEHAVIOR-NEUTRAL REWRITE
- C.ROUND_REPEAT_RATIONALE (shown on Briefing.html, "Why 5 rounds?") no
  longer implies that changing one's answer across rounds is the expected
  or more useful outcome (old text: "the purpose is to observe how your
  confidence and choices evolve"). New text states only that the data is
  static across rounds and asks for an independent judgment each round,
  without endorsing either repeating or changing one's choice — avoiding a
  framing effect on the very behavior (round-to-round choice stability)
  that the study measures.
- This is a wording-only mitigation for participant-facing framing. It does
  NOT address the underlying structural issue (suppliers/scores are static
  across rounds, so a motivated participant can compute the true optimum
  from round 1 data alone) — that remains an open, unresolved design
  question per Voice_125 (advisors: "serious... I have no answer right
  now"). AI-wrong-answer randomization (B/C/D/E instead of always E) was
  considered as a structural fix but explicitly deferred, not implemented.
